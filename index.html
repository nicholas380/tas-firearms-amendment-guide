Our principle

This guide does not tell you what to think.

It separates the official proposal, its practical meaning, potential benefits, potential concerns and unanswered questions.

  • Facts are labelled as facts.
  • Policy claims are not treated as proof.
  • Lawful use and criminal misuse are not automatically conflated.
  • Public safety and fairness are considered together.

Thinking beyond the headlines

A fair test for every proposed law

Fear can make any restriction sound reasonable. Familiarity can make any restriction sound unnecessary. A sound decision requires more than either reaction.

01

Define the problem

What harm is occurring, how often does it occur, and who or what is causing it?

02

Check the link

Would the proposed rule actually affect the behaviour or pathway producing that harm?

03

Measure the trade-off

What burden will fall on lawful people, businesses, police and courts?

04

Compare alternatives

Could storage, enforcement, intelligence, targeted eligibility rules or another measure work better?

Evidence

Data, legal text, documented incidents or official operational information.

Rationale

The reason offered for expecting a proposal to improve safety.

Open question

An important issue the published material does not yet resolve.
